Overall Aim of the Practicum Guide 
The practicum guide has been developed to guide tutors to 
effectively train  students in practicum sites to transfer 
theoretical knowledge into practice. It provides an 
opportunity for students to practice newly learned skills 
under the facilitation and supervision of tutors and clinical 
instructors. This practicum guide provides guidance on 
required areas for rotation, specific skills to be l earnt and 
tasks to be completed. This guide promotes standardization 
of clinical medicine  training in the p ractical and clinical 
settings.

Monitoring and Evaluation 
 The aim of monitoring and evaluation in this context is 
to check student progress in acquisition of skills. 
 Monitoring and evaluation will be done using checklists 
and end of assignment reports. 
 The checklist is a kind of task analysis describing what 
units of performance (step by step) are to be 
accomplished and the performance requirements 
associated with each step. 
 The checklist determines whether students follow 
accepted steps in accomplishing required criteria 
(tasks/steps), how accurately they perform them, and 
in what sequence. 
 Checklists shall be used to support the student to grow 
and develop towards desired levels of clinical 
competence instead of using it as a formal assessment 
tool.

Instructions for Completing Checklists 
 Each skill checklist contains a column on the far left 
side labelled ‘Standard’. The standards noted in this 
column relate to the overall procedure area. For each 
standard, there is an associated list of Criteria 
(steps/tasks) for students to complete in order to 
successfully meet the standard. 
 There are three columns in the checklist labelled: 
‘Observed’, ‘Not Observed’ and ‘Not Applicable.’ For 
each criterion, instructors/tutors must tick ONE of the 
three boxes according to the guidance below: 
o Tick (√) ‘Observed’ if a student performs the 
criterion (step/task) correctly and in the proper 
sequence.  
o Tick (√) ‘Not Observed’ if a student does not 
perform the criterion (step/task) correctly; OR does 
not perform the task in the proper sequence; OR is 
not observed performing a task that is able to be 
performed given the circumstances.  
 Note: Sometimes a criterion has sub-criteria. If 
there are multiple elements to a criterion and 
ANY part of the task is not observed, then ‘Not 
Observed’ should be ticked.

Section I: Overview of the Practicum 
Overall Aim of Clinical Training 
The overall aim of the clinical practice is to expose students 
to the administrativ e and clinical activities in the health 
facility. While rota ting through the different departments , 
students will acquire relevant skills to enable them to 
provide or participate in the delivery of ser vices at the 
primary health facility.  
The Purpose of the Practicum 
The practicum is  a clinical  attachment intended to give 
students opportunities for supervised practical application 
of theoretical concepts studied in class during the current 
or past semester.  
Clinical Rotation/Placement 
This is a form of practicum where students are attached to 
a clinical area for a specified period of time in order to learn 
the intended skills. At the site they have more interaction 
with patients and staff and have some responsibilities. 
During the clinical placement, responsibility for teaching 
relies on both clinical instructors and tutors. The practical 
time has to be utilized appropriately through organization of

classroom and clinical hours for students to have enough 
time for effective acquisition of skills. 
Administrative Field Attachment 
This is an off -college educational experience where 
students will be attached  to the Dispensary/Health Center 
in small manageable groups for two (2) weeks duration with 
an aim of exposing learners to administrative experiences 
at the primary healthcare facility.  
 
Structure and Clinical Practicum Areas 
During NTA level 6, semester I, students will have 11 hours 
a week in the practical/clinical  setting. Administrative Field 
Attachment will be conducted during  semester I, students 
will e ngage in 35  hours per week in a 2 -weeks period. 
During NTA level 6, semester II, students will have 16 hours 
a week in the clinical setting.

Section II: Clinical Teaching Team and their Roles 
Implementation of practical train ing requires the 
collaborative ef forts of tutors, clinical instructors  and 
students. Each of these groups has different roles in the 
teaching and learning process . The relationship between 
members of the clinical teaching team and students is 
important. Tu tors, clinical  instructors and students must 
communicate clearly with each other  during the learning 
process.  
Students will gain the most from their experiences when 
feedback is given constructively in the spirit of promoting 
learning. Students are not ex pected to know how to do 
everything perfectly at once, but rather, they are in the 
process of learning to improve upon their current skills and 
learn new ones. The clinical instructor or tutor will work with 
each student or a group of students during the p rocess of 
clinical learning and teaching.  
The tutor and clinical instructor  will be responsible to 
monitor, evaluate, and give constructive feedback on 
students’ learning. The students will have the opportunity to 
seek guidance from and provide feedback to the tutors and 
clinical instructors.

Section III: Clinical Competencies According to NTA 
Level Six (6) 
Introduction 
The NTA level 6 graduates (Holders of Ordinary Diploma in 
Clinical Medicine or Clinical Officers) are expected to work 
at the primary healthcare facility . The Clinical Officer is a 
frontline health worker who is the leader of the health team

maintain good public relations in order to mobilize various

answerable to the District Medical Officer. He/she works in 
close collaboration with village health committee. The key 
functions of the Clinical Officer include all the functions of a 
Clinical Assistant, plus the following: 
 Organizing, conducting and supervising the provision of 
preventive service at his/her catchment area.  
 Conducting simple survey in the community to identify 
health needs.

according to identified needs.

Activity 3: Obstetric procedures

e.g. obstetric wards and RCH clinic, they should carry out the 
following procedures as relevant to the clinical setting: 
 Perform manual removal of placenta  
 Perform bimanual compression of the uterus    
 Assist Caesarean section  
 Perform bedside investigations  
 Conduct normal delivery  
 Perform venepuncture  
 Insert urethral catheter  
 Administer blood  
 Administer oxygen therapy 
 Administer IV Fluids in relevant calibration  
 Perform cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) 
 Assist breech delivery  
 Repair first or second degree perineal tear

experience e.g. gynaecology wards, OPD and RCH clinic;  instruct

clinical setting:

patients with gynaecological conditions, and present

 Assist major gynaecological surgeries  
 Perform Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA)

Practicum  
 Refers to the part of a course consisting of practical 
work in a particular field. 
 The new curriculum for NTA level 4, 5 and 6 stresses 
on competency based training; it was found necessary 
to standardize both the classroom and the clinical 
training. 
 This overview attempts to create some guidance on 
how training should be standardized in the clinic and 
other settings where students will be attached. 
 Rotation/ Placement 
 A period in which students in the clinical part of their 
education passes through various clinical areas for 

===== PAGE 341 =====

 Practicum Guide for Tutors and Clinical Instructors  
NTA Level 6 Clinical Medicine  339 
 
short periods of time so that they have opportunity to 
learn the different aspects of care. 
 
Field Visit 
 A field visit is an off-school educational experience to 
students which normally involves travelling to a place 
where the students will acquire intended knowledge, 
experience and skills.  
 The field visit is intended to give an opportunity to 
students to experience the actual situation. The field 
visit usually lasts from a few hours to a day or days. 
 
Community Field Work 
 A community fieldwork is an off-school educational 
experience to students which normally involves 
travelling to a selected community setting. 
 Community field work aims to provide students with 
opportunities to work at a community level for health 
promotion, so that they can gain experience with 
assessing community needs, planning with the 
community, implementing the plan, and evaluating 
progress with the community.
